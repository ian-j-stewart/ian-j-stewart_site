Artificial intelligence is becoming a general-purpose technology for international security. It is being integrated into intelligence analysis, operational planning, command and control, and the production of open-source information. At the same time, it introduces new risks: the spread of dual-use capabilities, the acceleration of proliferation-relevant research, the difficulty of attributing outputs, and the potential for automation to compress decision-making timelines.

This research agenda examines how AI is being adopted in security institutions and what forms of governance are appropriate. It is not limited to a single threat or policy domain. It covers the use of large language models and other generative systems for research and analysis, the risks that such systems could lower barriers to chemical, biological, radiological or nuclear knowledge, and the organisational changes needed to use AI while preserving expert judgement.

A recurring theme is the gap between technical capability and institutional readiness. Many organisations are under pressure to adopt AI quickly, but the infrastructure for auditing, validating and contesting AI outputs remains uneven. This agenda asks what standards of provenance, uncertainty quantification and human accountability are practical, and how they can be embedded in workflows rather than treated as after-the-fact compliance checks.

The work also connects to broader debates about strategic stability. If AI is used to support early warning, targeting or crisis management, how will it affect the speed and interpretability of decisions? What are the risks of miscommunication or unintended escalation? And how can arms-control, confidence-building and risk-reduction measures adapt to a landscape in which some capabilities are privately controlled and globally distributed?

In practical terms, the agenda includes work on the application of AI to nonproliferation and export-control analysis, the evaluation of large language models for CBRN-relevant tasks, and the development of responsible frameworks for AI-enabled research and decision-support.
