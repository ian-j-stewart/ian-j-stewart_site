Dr Ian J. Stewart is Executive Director of the Washington, DC office of the James Martin Center for Nonproliferation Studies. An international-security scholar and practitioner, his research examines the governance of strategically consequential technologies: how states seek to control their diffusion, protect sensitive knowledge and infrastructure, and manage their effects on international security.

His work spans nuclear nonproliferation and deterrence, export controls, sanctions and proliferation finance, research security, semiconductor supply chains, compute governance and artificial intelligence.

Before joining CNS in 2020, Ian established and led [Project Alpha](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/research/project-alpha) at King's College London, developing it into a substantial research programme focused on countering proliferation-related trade. Earlier in his career, he worked for the United Kingdom Ministry of Defence as a nuclear engineer and assessor, including work on counterproliferation, nuclear-deterrent issues and the technical, end-use and end-user assessment of export-licence applications.

His academic and technical background includes a PhD in War Studies from King's College London, an MSc in Nuclear Science and Technology, and an MEng in Electrical and Electronic Engineering. He is the author of [*International Nuclear Export Controls and Non-Proliferation: The Collective Action Problem*](/publications/international-nuclear-export-controls-and-non-proliferation/) and co-editor, with Daniel Salisbury and Andrea Viski, of [*Preventing the Proliferation of WMDs: Measuring the Success of UN Security Council Resolution 1540*](/publications/preventing-the-proliferation-of-wmds/). He has authored or edited two books and numerous articles and reports, and has led or managed more than 25 funded projects worth over $20 million.

His current research extends his established work on technology controls and nonproliferation into artificial intelligence, compute governance, semiconductors, research security and the wider governance of strategic technology.
